## 1. Goal (as stated by the user)

- Optimise performance, and **change the architecture if needed**.
- **Do not fully trust the numbers already in the repository.** Derive the ceiling from hardware facts, then confirm it with measurements on the device.
- Order: first compute, from the hardware, the maximum frame rate for 320x180 audio+video; then optimise the real bottleneck, borrowing existing solutions where possible.
- Hardware first; video sources are out of scope for this phase.

## 2. Current playback path (from the code, not the old documents)

```
source -> ffmpeg (one decode, two outputs)
   video: fps=12 -> scale + pad to 320x180 -> rgb8 fixed 3-3-2 palette (no dither) -> raw index bytes
   audio: 16 kHz mono s16le, 40 ms = 1280 B per chunk
-> server/: frame cut into 15 stripes of 12 rows, each zlib level 6; packed to a byte budget (target 12 KB, max 22528 B);
   RateController picks the send rate from budget / bytes-per-frame; audio leads 320 ms, video 200 ms
-> one TCP connection (FAV1, 24-byte header) over Wi-Fi
-> ESP32-C3 device (160 MHz single core, no PSRAM)
   receive_task (prio 6): owns the socket, reads both streams in order; PCM queue 24 chunks (960 ms), stops reading above 20
                          (back-pressure); 2 x 22.5 KB packet buffers, a packet with no free buffer is read and discarded (nobuf)
   audio_task (prio 7): I2S playback and the session clock; plays silence on an empty queue for up to 3 s
   video_task (prio 5): drops a whole frame that is more than 100 ms behind the audio clock;
                        per stripe: ROM tinfl -> av_expand_indexed lookup to RGB565 -> OSD overlay -> SPI DMA; two stripe buffers pipelined
-> ST7789P3, 4-wire SPI at 40 MHz; the panel GRAM is the frame buffer
```

Code entry points: [`docs/assets/architecture-index.md`](../assets/architecture-index.md), sections 2 and 3.

## 4. Theoretical ceilings (320x180 = 57600 pixels)

Every figure is an **estimate** until section 8 confirms or refutes it.

### 4.1 Panel SPI

ms per frame = pixels x bits per pixel / SCLK + stripes x about 100 us of command and synchronisation overhead each.

| Format | SCLK | Stripes | ms/frame | fps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RGB565 | 40 MHz | 15 | 24.5 | **40.7** |
| RGB565 | 40 MHz | 4 | 23.4 | 42.7 |
| RGB444 | 40 MHz | 15 | 18.8 | 53.2 |
| RGB565 | 80 MHz (**out of panel spec**) | 15 | 13.0 | 76.8 |

**In spec, a full-frame refresh tops out at about 41 fps.** Only sending changed regions or a smaller pixel format goes past it.

### 4.2 CPU

About 136 M cycles/s are available (160 MHz less 15% for the Wi-Fi driver and interrupts). Cycles per frame = receive cycles/byte x bytes + inflate x pixels + lookup expand x pixels + about 1.5 ms of SPI scheduling.

| Scheme | Optimistic (20/25/8 c) | Conservative (36/40/14 c) |
|---|---|---|
| Current: 8-bit index + zlib, ratio about 3 | 53.6 fps | **33.4 fps** |
| 8-bit index + zlib, ratio about 6 | 58.0 | 36.5 |
| 8-bit index, uncompressed | 73.1 | 43.2 |
| RGB444 pass-through | 68.8 | 40.2 |
| RGB565 pass-through | 53.2 | 30.7 |

The bracketed figures are receive cycles/byte, inflate cycles/byte and expand cycles/pixel, **estimated** from typical RISC-V costs for lwIP, tinfl and table lookup, **not measured**. In the conservative case, zlib inflate is about 55% of each frame's CPU.

JPEG: Espressif's `esp_new_jpeg` publishes no C3 figures. It decodes 320x240 at 25.6 fps on the ESP32-S2 (240 MHz single core); scaled to the C3 and to 320x180 that is roughly 15-20 fps with the CPU saturated, leaving nothing for receive. **Not recommended.**

### 4.3 Wi-Fi link

fps = (link rate - 32 KB/s audio) / bytes per frame.

| Link | raw RGB565 | raw 8-bit index | index + zlib ratio 3 | index + zlib ratio 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 MB/s | 8.4 | 16.8 | 50.4 | 100.8 |
| 2.0 MB/s | 17.1 | 34.2 | 102.5 | 205.0 |

Even at the official minimum rank (about 2.2 MB/s), halved to 1 MB/s, the link is not the bottleneck. **Current use is about 0.2 MB/s, roughly a tenth of what the hardware offers.**

### 4.4 Combined

CPU and SPI DMA can overlap, so the ceiling is about min(panel ~41, CPU 33-54, link ~50) = **35-40 fps**, with 25-30 fps a realistic target. Playback today is 5-12 fps; the gap is software and protocol, not hardware.

## 5. Where the gap comes from (found while reading the code)

1. **The server limits itself.** [`server/rate.py`](../../server/rate.py) defaults `VIDEO_BUDGET_BYTES` to 185000 (185 KB/s), which at about 19 KB a frame is about 9.7 fps. The `state-20260916` document records the shipped value as 120000, which disagrees with the code; **the code wins**.
2. **The device receive path is fragile.** One task reads both streams in order, so it has head-of-line blocking; there are only 2 fixed packet buffers; a frame must fit in one packet. Any jitter becomes a dropped frame or starved audio.
3. **The Wi-Fi config is below the official minimum rank.** See section 3.
4. **Memory is tied up.** The TV firmware still sets `CONFIG_BT_ENABLED=y`; LVGL and the demo code are still compiled through `main.c` (not yet confirmed whether the linker drops their static memory); cJSON parsing the channel list peaks at about 31 KB.
5. **Stale documents; do not rely on them.**
   - [`docs/local-tv-prototype.md`](../local-tv-prototype.md) still describes 160x120 JPEG and 20 ms audio.
   - The `AV_RAW_PROTOTYPE` help text in `main/Kconfig.projbuild` still says JPEG.
   - The header comment in [`server/frames.py`](../../server/frames.py) says 15 stripes of 16 rows, while the current default `TV_GEOMETRY=320x180` is 15 stripes of 12 rows.

## 6. Questions discussed and settled

- **LVGL**: no benefit for video. The C3 has no 2D accelerator, so LVGL output crosses the same 40 MHz SPI; it costs about 34 KB more RAM, and it cannot share the panel with raw mode. The existing `ui_text`/`ui_menu`, which paint text into stripes on their way out, suit this chip better. LVGL is only useful for setup or settings pages while nothing is playing.
- **Interlacing**: cannot break the ceiling. The SPI limit is pixel throughput (40 MHz / 16 bit = 2.5 Mpixel/s). The ST7789 has no interlaced write: skipping rows means resetting the window for every row, 4 SPI transactions each, adding 3.6-9 ms per field, for only 49-66 fields/s in practice. The panel's own refresh is about 60 Hz and there is no TE pin, which makes it worse. The `SM` bit in `GATECTRL(E4h)` only changes the panel's internal gate scan order and does not reduce SPI data. An LCD also has no phosphor decay like a CRT, so interlacing shows combing on motion rather than an old-TV look.
- **"Scanline" mode** (candidate, not implemented): the server sends 320x90; the device expands each row into one bright line and one line at 25% brightness. Network bytes and CPU are halved, SPI is unchanged, and it gives a CRT look. The cost is half the vertical detail and coarser captions, so it should be a switchable mode.
- **The two projects the user supplied are ruled out**:
  - [aquaticus/esp32_composite_video_lib](https://github.com/aquaticus/esp32_composite_video_lib): generates analog PAL/NTSC composite video from the classic ESP32 DAC (GPIO25). The C3 is RISC-V with no DAC, has no free pin, would need a frame buffer of about 110 KB, is monochrome only, GPL licensed, and targets an external CRT rather than the built-in LCD.
  - [wooky/esp32-analog-video-over-wifi](https://github.com/wooky/esp32-analog-video-over-wifi): the opposite direction, a capture and send side (analog camera -> decoder chip -> I2S -> JPEG -> Wi-Fi); needs >= 1 MB PSRAM and two cores; not updated since 2020.

## 8. On-device benchmark plan

Principle: measure one stage at a time, with the others switched off or fed synthetic data, so the link or the sender's pacing cannot hide the result.

### 8.0 Prerequisites

- **The previous machine had no ESP-IDF** (no `idf.py`, empty `IDF_PATH`). On the new machine, install ESP-IDF 5.5.3 following [`environment-setup.md`](engineering/environment-setup.md), **with the user's permission first**. The PC also needs **iperf 2.x** (not iperf3; the Espressif example only speaks iperf2).
- **Take two identical full-flash backups before writing the device:**

  ```bash
  esptool.py -p <PORT> read_flash 0 0x800000 backup1.bin
  esptool.py -p <PORT> read_flash 0 0x800000 backup2.bin
  # the two SHA256 hashes must match
  ```

- Flash test firmware **only with `idf.py -p <PORT> app-flash`** (the application partition at 0x10000), which leaves `cardid@0x356000` and the Wi-Fi settings in NVS alone. **No full erase and no merged image written from 0x0.**
- This project's flashing and serial workflow **has never been run on Windows**; expect some troubleshooting the first time.

### 8.1 Step 1: raw Wi-Fi throughput (existing tool, no code)

- Use the ESP-IDF example `examples/wifi/iperf`. Before building, substitute this project's `partitions.csv` and 8 MB flash settings, then `app-flash`.
- After joining the router from the device console, run `iperf -s -i 3` on the device and `iperf -c <device IP> -i 3 -t 30` on the PC (TCP receive). For UDP, use `iperf -s -u` with `iperf -c ... -u -b 30M`.
- Run two sdkconfigs, three 30-second runs each, recording RSSI and channel:
  - the example's own iperf rank: this board's physical ceiling in this environment;
  - this project's Wi-Fi/lwIP settings: how much the current configuration gives away.
- Flash the original firmware back afterwards.

### 8.2 Step 2: panel and CPU (no network, new benchmark firmware)

Extend the existing `CONFIG_AV_DISPLAY_BENCH` (`display_bench()` in `main/av_player.c`) into a new Kconfig option `CONFIG_AV_HW_BENCH`, default off. It runs everything at boot before the network starts and prints one `BENCH name=... key=value` line per item for a script to tabulate.

**Panel** (320x180 window, 120 frames per item):

| Variant | What it answers |
|---|---|
| 12-row stripes x 15 `draw_bitmap` calls (current) | Baseline |
| 30-row and 60-row stripes | Fixed cost per stripe |
| Set the window once, then stream DMA with `tx_color(io, -1, ...)` | How close to the 23 ms theory |
| COLMOD 12 bit (RGB444) | Gain from 25% fewer bytes, and whether it displays correctly |
| (optional) SPI 80 MHz | **Out of ST7789 spec**; the user decides, not included by default |

**CPU** (cycles via `esp_cpu_get_cycle_count()`, synthetic stripes of controlled entropy): `memcpy` baseline (cycles/byte); ROM `tinfl` cycles per output byte at compression ratios 2, 3, 5 and 8; current `av_expand_indexed` cycles/pixel; optimised expand (16-bit writes + pre-swapped palette + IRAM); a 4x4 block decoder prototype (MS Video-1 style).

### 8.3 Step 3: CPU cost of receiving (the product's own network stack)

The least certain estimate (20-36 cycles/byte), and it directly sets the CPU ceiling.

- **Device**: a lowest-priority idle-counter task, calibrated with the device quiet; then receive normally, counting bytes without processing them. The drop in the idle count is the CPU spent receiving.
- **PC**: a new `tools/hw_bench_link.py` sending at 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 MB/s, 20 s per step, in 1.4 KB, 8 KB and 22 KB blocks.
- **Result**: a throughput-to-CPU curve whose slope is receive cycles/byte.

### 8.4 Step 4: end-to-end knee (existing tool)

Drive [`tools/transport_probe.py`](../../tools/transport_probe.py) at fixed rates of 6, 9, 12, 15, 20 and 25 fps, 60 s per step, reading the device `CLOCK_ESTIMATED` log for achieved rate, `late`, `nobuf` and `AUDIO_EMPTY`. The step where the rate stops rising and frames start dropping is the real ceiling of the current architecture. Field meanings are in [`metrics-dictionary.md`](metrics-dictionary.md).

### 8.5 Combining the results

```
fps_panel = 1 / per-frame time of the best step-2 panel variant
fps_cpu   = available cycles / (receive c/B x bytes per frame + inflate + expand)
fps_link  = (step-1 or step-3 throughput - 32 KB/s) / bytes per frame
ceiling   = min(all three)
```

Compare with step 4: a wide gap means the software architecture has room; a narrow one means the hardware floor has been reached.

### 8.6 Division of work

- **AI**: the step 2 and step 3 firmware (default-off Kconfig options), `hw_bench_link.py`, a log summary script, and setup notes for the step 1 iperf example.
- **User**: permission to install ESP-IDF and iperf2, connecting the device, confirming the backup, flashing, and supplying serial logs.

## 9. Optimisation candidates after benchmarking (by expected gain)

> **RE-RANKED 2026-09-29 from measurement.** Item 8 was ranked last on the
> assumption that the CPU might be the bottleneck; it is now **first**, because
> the panel is and this is what removes its per-stripe cost. Items 6 and 5 drop,
> for the reasons given. Items 1, 2, 3, 4 and 7 are unchanged in rank and remain
> unmeasured.
>
> New item 9 is the largest single lever found and the only one that cannot be
> adopted on the evidence available.

1. **Bypass esp_lcd** (was item 8): one window per frame instead of fifteen.
   The product pays 30 ms a frame against a 23.04 ms floor and the whole 7 ms
   difference is this. A coarser stripe height alone recovers most of it for one
   constant — 30 ms to 24 ms — and the window completes it to 23 ms.
2. **Send only changed stripes or blocks**: the panel GRAM keeps the last frame,
   so the device needs no reference-frame memory; a periodic full frame provides
   self-healing. A wire protocol change, so update all four places listed in
   section 5 of the architecture index.
3. **Rework receive**: following pocket-tv, streaming inflate with a shared ring
   buffer, and no longer requiring a frame to fit in one packet.
4. **Free memory**: disable Bluetooth, drop LVGL and the demo code, replace the
   cJSON channel list with a compact format; give the space to receive buffers.
5. **IMA ADPCM audio** (4:1): about 13% more link for video; the PCM queue
   shrinks from about 30 KB to about 8 KB, enough for another video buffer or a
   deeper audio buffer. Unchanged, and still not on the critical path.
6. ~~**Wi-Fi settings at least at the official default rank**~~ **Dropped.**
   Measured: the product's own parameters give up 6% (31.6 against 33.7 Mbit/s),
   and the product uses about 0.2 MB/s of it. About 2 Mbit/s is not worth
   chasing against a 33 fps panel ceiling.
7. ~~**A new codec**~~ **Dropped.** Step 2 measured the CPU at about 66 fps of
   headroom against a 33 fps panel ceiling, and receiving at 2.6% of the CPU. A
   4x4 block codec would reduce cycles that are not binding.
8. **Scanline mode**: see section 6. Unchanged.
9. **NEW — the 80 MHz panel clock.** Halves the frame time: 30 ms to 16 ms,
   23 ms to 12 ms. Larger than items 1 through 7 combined. **But it is outside
   the panel's rating on two independent counts, and nobody has looked at a
   screen driven that way** — the measurement says what the bus spent, not that
   the picture was right. Flash `tools/sdkconfig.hw-bench-spi80` and look before
   believing it.
